Research

Research Areas & Capabilities

Our group investigates coupled biogeochemical processes connecting soil minerals, organic matter, microorganisms, nutrients, and water quality. Below is a high-level overview of our active research directions and laboratory infrastructure.

16:10 CO₂ Mitigation & Carbon Sequestration images/research-co2.jpg

Harnessing Natural Phenomena for CO₂ Mitigation

We evaluate natural geochemical pathways for carbon sequestration, spanning enhanced silicate mineral weathering in soils and the persistence of organic matter under anoxic marine conditions.

HUJI–UIUC (Prof. Yuji Arai) EU AgroServ US–Israel BSF (Prof. Malak Tfaily, Univ. of Arizona)
16:10 Mineral–Organic Matter Resilience images/research-maom.jpg

Mineral–Organic Matter Resilience & Environmental Transitions

Focusing on iron mineral–organic matter assemblages, we investigate how aging and shifting salinity or redox gradients govern the stability and fate of carbon moving from terrestrial to aquatic systems.

ISF Einstein Foundation (Prof. Mina Bizic, TU Berlin)
16:10 Soil Nitrogen Cycling & N₂O images/research-n2o.jpg

Abiotic & Biotic Controls on N₂O Emissions

We study the interplay between chemical and microbial pathways driving nitrogen oxide formation in soils, particularly during dynamic moisture and redox fluctuations.

HUJI–BGU Partnership for Sustainability (Prof. Ilya Gelfand)
16:10 Phosphorus Recovery & Vivianite images/research-vivianite.jpg

Phosphorus Recovery & Vivianite Precipitation

We evaluate sustainable nutrient recovery by investigating iron-phosphate (vivianite) mineral precipitation pathways and geochemical controls in complex aqueous environments.

Dr. Maya Engel

Joined HUJI in October 2023 · maya.engel@mail.huji.ac.il

Dr. Maya Engel leads the BioGeoChem Lab in the Department of Soil and Water Sciences at The Robert H. Smith Faculty of Agriculture, Food and Environment, The Hebrew University of Jerusalem.

Prior to joining HUJI in October 2023, she completed her postdoctoral studies at Stanford University and SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory (Fendorf Group & John Bargar). She earned her Ph.D. in Environmental Chemistry from the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (Chefetz Group) and her B.Sc. in Chemistry from Tel Aviv University.

Teaching & Mentoring

Mentoring Philosophy

As an environmental chemist and educator, Dr. Maya Engel views mentorship as a cornerstone of scientific discovery. Our lab fosters an inclusive, collaborative environment built on hands-on experimentation, independent critical thinking, and open interdisciplinary dialogue.

We are deeply committed to supporting every student's individual trajectory—from undergraduate project students taking their first steps at the bench to Ph.D. candidates developing independent research programs—and to actively advocating for Women in Science across academia and environmental fields.

Current Opening

Open M.Sc. Position — Phosphorus Recovery & Vivianite Precipitation

We are seeking a motivated M.Sc. student to join our group in Rehovot to investigate sustainable phosphorus recovery through iron-phosphate (vivianite) mineral precipitation in complex aqueous systems. Relevant backgrounds: Chemistry, Soil & Water Sciences, Environmental Sciences, Earth Sciences, Chemical Engineering, or related fields.
