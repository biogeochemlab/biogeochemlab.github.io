BioGeoChem Lab

Led by Dr. Maya Engel at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, our lab explores coupled biogeochemical cycles at the mineral–organic–microbial interface. We seek to understand how fundamental interfacial chemistry governs carbon persistence, nutrient availability, and environmental resilience in soils and sediments.

What We Study

Core Research Themes

Organo-Mineral Interactions

Understanding how organic matter associates with mineral surfaces to influence carbon stability and soil structure across scales.

Redox & Elemental Cycling

Exploring how redox-active minerals and microbial activity couple the transformations of iron, carbon, nitrogen, and essential nutrients.

Soil & Environmental Resilience

Linking molecular-level soil chemistry to sustainable agriculture, water quality, and climate adaptation in changing environments.

Research

Research Areas & Capabilities

Our research bridges fundamental biogeochemical processes with real-world soil and water challenges. Below is a high-level overview of our active thematic areas and laboratory capabilities.

16:10 [Photo: Mineral–Organic Interfaces]

Organo-Mineral Stabilization

We study how organic compounds interact with soil minerals to regulate long-term carbon storage. Our focus is on uncovering the broad chemical drivers that make soil carbon persistent or vulnerable under shifting environmental conditions.

Carbon Cycling Einstein Foundation
16:10 [Photo: Redox & Iron Systems]

Redox Dynamics in Soils & Sediments

Fluctuating oxygen levels reshape mineral phases and drive coupled elemental transformations. We examine how iron and manganese cycling interact with organic matter and trace elements across oxic–anoxic transitions.

Redox Biogeochemistry BSF
16:10 [Photo: Nutrient & Mineral Formation]

Nutrient Cycling & Resource Recovery

Essential nutrients such as phosphorus and nitrogen are tightly linked to mineral formation and dissolution. We investigate pathways that support sustainable nutrient retention and recovery in managed ecosystems.

Nutrient Dynamics ISF
16:10 [Photo: Agricultural & Semi-Arid Soils]

Sustainable Agroecosystems & Water Quality

Working across semi-arid and agricultural landscapes, we examine how soil amendments and land management influence soil health, water quality, and the fate of environmental contaminants.

Agroecosystems EU AgroServ · International Partners

Principal Investigator

Dr. Maya Engel

Department of Soil and Water Sciences · Faculty of Agriculture, HUJI (Rehovot)

Dr. Maya Engel is a faculty member in the Department of Soil and Water Sciences at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem. Her research investigates the chemical and biological processes operating at mineral–organic–water interfaces, connecting fundamental environmental chemistry with soil sustainability and biogeochemical cycling.

More About Maya

Beyond the lab, Maya wears many hats: Scientist, Educator, Mentor, Mother, Runner, and Women in Science advocate. She believes great science grows out of curiosity, kindness, open dialogue, and a supportive team culture.

Mentoring is central to everything we do. We aim to foster an inclusive, intellectually curious, and supportive environment where students develop independent scientific thinking, strong analytical judgment, and clear communication skills.

Current Opportunities

Open MSc / PhD Positions in Soil & Environmental Biogeochemistry

We are recruiting motivated graduate students to join our team at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem (Rehovot campus) to work on interdisciplinary projects at the intersection of soil chemistry, mineral–organic interactions, redox processes, and nutrient cycling. Specific research directions are tailored together with each student based on shared interests and background.

Relevant backgrounds: Chemistry, Soil & Water Sciences, Environmental Sciences, Earth Sciences, or related fields.
